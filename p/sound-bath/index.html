Sound baths, gong baths & sound healing in London

A sound bath is an hour lying on the floor while someone plays gongs, singing bowls, chimes and voice around the room. There is nothing to do: the sound is long and overlapping, and most people drift between waking and sleep.

London's sound baths run in yoga studios, churches and railway arches, often in the early evening. Bring warm socks — you cool down quickly lying still — and expect to leave slightly dazed.

You'll also see them billed as sound healing, sound meditation, sound journeys or vibrational sound therapy: in a group setting these are all much the same thing, led with gongs, crystal and Tibetan singing bowls, chimes, drums and voice. Many people come for the rest itself — to switch off, sleep better or let go of a stressful week.

Common questions

What is a sound bath?
A group session where you lie down and listen to sustained acoustic sound — usually gongs and crystal or Tibetan bowls — for forty-five to sixty minutes. 'Bath' refers to being surrounded by sound, not to water; nothing is asked of you beyond lying still.
What should I bring to a sound bath?
Warm layers and socks, and a blanket if the listing doesn't say one is provided. Most venues supply mats, bolsters and eye masks. Arrive early enough to settle, as latecomers are often held at the door once the gongs start.
Is a gong bath the same as a sound bath?
A gong bath is a sound bath led mainly by gongs, which are louder and more physical than bowls — you feel them in your chest. Sessions billed as sound healing or sound journeys use a wider mix of instruments and often voice.
What's the difference between a sound bath and sound therapy?
A sound bath is a group session: everyone lies down and the practitioner plays for the room. Sound therapy usually means a one-to-one treatment, such as a singing bowl massage where bowls are placed on or around your body. Group sessions are cheaper and need no booking ahead beyond a ticket; this page lists the group ones.
How long is a sound bath?
Most London sound baths last 45 to 75 minutes of sound, with time either side to settle in and come round — so allow an hour and a half. Lunchtime and early-morning sessions can be as short as half an hour, and all-night gong journeys run until dawn.
Can a sound bath help with sleep and stress?
Many people find them deeply relaxing and sleep well afterwards, and small studies have reported lower tension and better mood after a session. They aren't a medical treatment, but as an hour of enforced rest with no screen and nothing to do, they work for a lot of people.
How much does a sound bath cost in London?
